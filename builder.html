Trust Overview

Begin by providing the fundamental details that will identify and define your express private trust.

The formal name of your trust as it will appear on all documentation.
Example: "The [Your Surname] Family Trust" or "The [Purpose] Trust 2025"
Why this matters: The trust name is used to formally identify the trust in all legal documents, correspondence, and records.
The date on which this trust deed is to be dated.
Why this matters: The creation date establishes when the trust legally comes into existence.
A plain-English statement of the primary purpose of this trust.
Why this matters: The purpose clause defines the intention of the trust and guides Trustees in their administration.
The legal jurisdiction whose laws will govern this trust.
Why this matters: Trust law varies significantly between jurisdictions. The governing law affects how the trust is administered and interpreted.
The property or nominal sum being settled into the trust at its creation.
Example: "£10 settled sum" (a nominal amount is typical for initial settlement)
Why this matters: A trust requires that property be transferred to it. An initial nominal sum is commonly used to constitute the trust before further assets are added.
